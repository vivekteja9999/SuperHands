01 — What actually happens

Five steps between your prompt and a running company.

It's a sequence on purpose — nothing gets built on a guess, and nothing goes live without you knowing what it can do.

01
You describe it
A website, an app, internal software — for your business or a new idea. Write it the way you'd explain it to a person, not a spec sheet.
02
We ask what you didn't say
A handful of follow-up questions until your requirements are actually confirmed, not guessed at from one sentence.
03
It gets built
Your product gets built and checked against what running it will actually require — not just what it takes to demo it once.
04
You get staffed
AI agents matched to what your product needs — support, bookings, billing, whatever applies — ready to work from the first day it's live.
05
You run it
Talk to your agents the way you'd talk to staff, put humans anywhere in the chain of command, and keep final say wherever you want it.

03 — What's underneath it

The parts that make a company runnable, not just built.

Memory at every level

The founder, the human manager, and each agent keep their own memory and their own slice of company knowledge — so context doesn't blur between them.

Everything logged, nothing hidden

Every action an agent takes is recorded and traceable back to who approved it. Oversight isn't an add-on setting — it's just how the system works.

Human sign-off, wherever you want it

Pick the decisions that need a person — a refund past a certain amount, a legal page, a hire — and it gets routed to the right one automatically.

An org chart you'd actually recognize

Put a human above an agent, or let an agent report to another agent. Modeled the way you already think about your team, not a flat dashboard.

Fix things in code or in plain English

Engineers on your team can edit the actual workflow. Everyone else can just describe the change, and the agent rewrites its own logic to match.

You're still the one in charge

Every agent and every human role answers up the same chain. That chain always ends with you, no matter how much of the day-to-day runs on its own.

04 — Changing how an agent behaves

Say it in code, or just say it.

Your engineers can go straight to the workflow. Everyone else can just tell the agent what's wrong — either way, the change actually takes.

Code-level

// refunds-agent/workflow.ts
on("refund.requested", (ctx) => {
  if (ctx.amount > 5000) {
    return routeToHuman("studio_manager");
  }
  return approveRefund(ctx);
});

Direct, versioned access to the actual agent logic — for the people on your team who'd rather just write it themselves.
